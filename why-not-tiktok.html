Official Studio Statement // October 2026

Why Untrusted Network Is Not on TikTok

Our work is public. Our principles are not for sale.

Untrusted Network is an independent game development studio. We believe interactive technology and creative media should serve human curiosity, craft and meaningful agency, rather than exploit cognitive and behavioural vulnerabilities.

For these reasons, we have chosen not to maintain an official presence on TikTok.

This is a deliberate ethical decision. It reflects our concerns regarding recommendation architectures designed around compulsive engagement, documented consequences for youth mental health and attention, aggressive data harvesting practices, and the erosion of transparency in algorithmic distribution. These concerns are informed by established independent research, regulatory findings and public enforcement actions across multiple jurisdictions.

We do not expect every creator or studio to make this choice. We do believe our community deserves an honest, transparent explanation of why we have made it.

01. Compulsive design and the engineering of attention

TikTok's recommendation engine is not an incidental feature; it is an optimization system engineered to capture and sustain human attention at maximum density. By pairing variable reward schedules with infinite, frictionless vertical scrolling and rapid content cycles, the platform maximizes user dwell time and session frequency.

Independent research into algorithmic feed dynamics indicates that hyper-personalized micro-targeting creates feedback loops that can diminish attentional autonomy, encourage compulsive consumption patterns, and degrade deep cognitive focus.

We believe technology should respect the human mind, not treat human attention as a resource to be extracted and monetized without regard for the user's wellbeing.

Our principle

Software design should respect human attention, not engineer behavioural dependence.

02. Youth wellbeing and cognitive development

Games and digital media are central to youth culture. Platforms that host large numbers of children and adolescents bear an heightened moral and legal responsibility towards their psychological development and safety.

In 2023, the US Surgeon General issued an advisory detailing profound concerns regarding social media use and youth mental health, noting correlations between excessive short-form algorithmic consumption and elevated rates of anxiety, depression, body image distortion, and severe sleep disruption among adolescents.

In the European Union, the European Commission initiated formal proceedings under the Digital Services Act to examine TikTok's behavioural design, including the addictive effects of its reward mechanics and its impact on the physical and mental wellbeing of minors.

We refuse to build an audience by feeding our creative work into a machine whose commercial incentives are aligned with cultivating vulnerability in young people.

Our principle

Commercial reach must never come at the expense of the wellbeing of children and young people.

03. Data harvesting, telemetry and surveillance

The extent and depth of data collection required to train modern recommendation engines poses fundamental questions about digital privacy and personal sovereignty.

In April 2023, the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) fined TikTok £12.7 million for multiple breaches of data protection law, specifically finding that the platform allowed an estimated 1.4 million children under 13 to use the service without parental consent and processed their personal data unlawfully.

Security researchers and regulatory bodies have also documented extensive telemetry gathering, device fingerprinting, and the integration of in-app web browsers capable of logging external keystrokes and link interactions.

We do not believe that access to cultural content should require visitors to submit to pervasive personal surveillance.

Our principle

Privacy is an inherent human right, not a currency to be bartered for digital distribution.

04. Algorithmic opacity and cultural control

When a single algorithmic black box controls what tens of millions of people see, learn and discuss each day, accountability and transparency become non-negotiable requirements for public trust.

TikTok operates with profound opacity. Independent researchers, civil society observers, and journalists face severe technical and institutional obstacles when attempting to audit its recommendation algorithms, verify content moderation decisions, or investigate systemic suppression.

Centralized corporate discretion over what becomes visible and what disappears from public view undermines the democratic nature of culture and the open web.

Our principle

Platforms that shape public culture must be open to independent scrutiny and democratic accountability.

05. Why this matters to an independent game studio

Our studio is creating [MODE-ZERO]—a side-on cyberpunk action game set in Nullgrid, a city ruled by corporate conglomerates and pervasive surveillance apparatuses like Keystone and Overwatch.

The themes of our game examine what happens when powerful entities commodify human identity, track every movement, and reduce citizens to data points. It would be fundamentally hypocritical for us to critique these dynamics in our art while enthusiastically feeding our work, community and marketing into corporate platforms that practice them in reality.

We recognize that many players and fellow creators use TikTok. Our decision is not a personal criticism of individual users. We simply believe that studios have the freedom and the duty to draw their own boundaries.

Our position

Untrusted Network will not maintain an official presence on TikTok.

We choose to build our studio's future through open, transparent, and respectful channels: direct RSS and static web dispatches, video archives on YouTube, and community conversations on decentralized and open platforms such as Bluesky.

We would rather connect with a smaller audience of thoughtful, engaged players on respectful terms than chase ephemeral viral metrics on platforms whose business models we cannot support.

We make games. We choose our principles. We stand by our decisions.

Untrusted Network
