Official Studio Statement // October 2026

Why Untrusted Network Is Not on X

Our work is public. Our principles are not for sale.

Untrusted Network is an independent game development studio. We believe technology should be developed and operated with responsibility, transparency, respect for people and accountability for those who wield power.

For these reasons, we have chosen not to maintain an official presence on X, formerly Twitter.

This is a deliberate ethical decision. It reflects our concerns about the platform's ownership, employment practices, content moderation, political influence, treatment of critics and approach to transparency. These concerns are informed by documented events, regulatory findings, independent research and court decisions.

We do not expect everyone to reach the same conclusion. We do believe that the decision deserves an honest explanation.

01. The human cost of corporate decisions

Following Elon Musk's acquisition of Twitter in 2022, approximately 3,700 employees were laid off in an initial round of redundancies. Further departures followed. The restructuring was accompanied by legal disputes concerning severance payments and employment rights.

The subsequent legal proceedings, including a claim by former senior executives over alleged unpaid severance, illustrate the human and legal consequences that can accompany decisions made at the highest levels of a technology company.

We recognise that businesses must sometimes restructure. However, the way those decisions are handled—and how affected individuals are treated—reveals whether corporate power is exercised with genuine accountability.

We believe employees are people, not disposable components in a business strategy.

Our principle

Commercial success does not remove an organisation's responsibility to treat people fairly.

02. Hate speech, moderation and user safety

A platform that hosts public conversation has responsibilities towards the people who use it and those affected by what happens there.

Independent research has raised concerns about X's handling of hateful content. The Anti-Defamation League's 2024 social media assessment, for example, identified significant shortcomings in X's handling of reported antisemitic content.

In December 2025, the European Commission fined X €120 million for breaches of the Digital Services Act relating to its blue-checkmark design, advertising transparency and researcher access to public data.

In 2026, Ofcom also investigated concerns relating to illegal content and the use of Grok to generate sexualised imagery, including imagery involving children. We distinguish an ongoing investigation from a final finding of liability, but consider the issues serious enough to warrant scrutiny.

We do not believe that freedom of expression means a platform should be indifferent to the consequences of its design, policies or enforcement decisions.

Our principle

Freedom of expression and responsibility for the safety of users must be taken seriously together.

03. Political influence and control of public discourse

X is not simply a place to post messages. Its recommendation systems influence what millions of people see, discuss and believe deserves their attention.

Elon Musk is both the platform's owner and a highly influential participant in political debate. His public endorsements and promotion of political movements raise legitimate questions about the concentration of power when an individual can participate in public discourse while also controlling a major channel through which that discourse is distributed.

Investigations into X's recommendations have also raised questions about political content and the visibility given to particular viewpoints. Individual studies have limitations, and we do not claim that every user's feed is the same or that any single platform determines election results.

Nevertheless, the potential for a platform owner to influence public attention deserves independent scrutiny.

We believe people should be able to understand how important digital public spaces operate, how recommendations shape their experience and what safeguards exist against the arbitrary exercise of power.

Our principle

Influence over public conversation should be accompanied by transparency and accountability.

04. Transparency and independent research

Independent researchers, journalists and civil society organisations play an essential role in identifying online abuse, coordinated manipulation, disinformation and other harms.

Following Musk's takeover, X substantially changed its API access arrangements, ending much of the free access previously available to developers and researchers. The introduction of paid access and restrictions on data access created practical barriers for some independent investigations.

X's access arrangements have also featured in legal disputes involving researchers and civil society organisations.

In 2024, a US federal judge dismissed X's lawsuit against the Center for Countering Digital Hate, an organisation that had published research critical of the platform's handling of hate speech. The court's decision included a finding that the lawsuit was motivated by disagreement with the organisation's criticism and concern about its impact on X's business.

We believe powerful companies should be open to legitimate scrutiny, including scrutiny that is uncomfortable or commercially inconvenient.

Our principle

Independent research and criticism are necessary safeguards, not inconveniences to be suppressed.

05. Corporate power and accountability

In 2024, Brazil's Supreme Court suspended X following a dispute over compliance with court orders and the requirement to maintain a legal representative in the country. X subsequently complied with the requirements necessary to resume service.

The dispute involved complex questions concerning legal authority, freedom of expression and the responsibilities of global technology platforms. We do not suggest that every government restriction is justified, or that a company must unquestioningly accept every demand made of it.

We do believe, however, that powerful technology companies must operate within applicable law and engage responsibly with legitimate legal processes.

Accountability must apply to institutions, governments and corporations alike.

Our principle

No company should be beyond scrutiny, and no claim of principle should exempt an organisation from its responsibilities.

06. Why this matters to an independent game studio

We make games because we believe technology can be a source of creativity, discovery, enjoyment and meaningful human connection.

We want to build our community in ways that reflect those values. That means considering not only where our work can reach an audience, but also the organisations and systems through which we choose to distribute it.

An official presence on a social media platform is not a neutral decision in every circumstance. It can direct attention, participation and commercial value towards that platform.

We recognise that people use X for many different reasons, including journalism, activism, professional communication and keeping in touch with others. Our decision is not a condemnation of everyone who uses it.

Nor do we claim that every other social media platform is free from ethical problems.

We simply believe that, when the concerns surrounding a particular platform conflict with our principles, we are entitled to choose not to participate.

Our position

Untrusted Network will not maintain an official presence on X while our ethical concerns remain unresolved.

This is our decision, made on the basis of the public record and our own judgement. We do not ask our audience to accept our conclusions without question. We encourage people to read the evidence, examine the arguments and make up their own minds.

We believe that independent creators and small studios should be able to choose where they participate without treating the commercial reach of a platform as more important than their principles.

We would rather build our community through channels that we are comfortable supporting than maintain a presence somewhere simply because it is influential.

We make games. We choose our principles. We stand by our decisions.
